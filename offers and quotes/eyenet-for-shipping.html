About Eyenet for Shipping

Ships are isolated, safety-critical environments operating far from shore-based management. Eyenet brings computer vision-powered crew presence, restricted area protection, and automated multi-level notifications to every vessel — then connects the entire fleet through a secure VPN mesh for centralised oversight.

The maritime industry faces unique operational challenges that off-the-shelf security systems cannot address. A vessel at sea is a self-contained world: crew must be accounted for at all times, watch stations must never be left unattended, restricted zones must be enforced 24/7, and all of this must function with zero internet dependency. Eyenet's on-premises, edge-computing architecture is purpose-built for exactly this environment — each vessel runs a complete, self-sufficient EyeNet installation on a single mini PC, operating fully offline at sea and synchronising data with fleet headquarters when docked or via satellite link.

The Maritime Operational Challenge

Offline Core Operation

Ships operate without internet for days or weeks at sea. All presence tracking, alerting, and access control must function locally — no cloud dependency for core operation. Data syncs automatically when connectivity resumes.

Safety Critical Environment

Know exactly who is onboard, who is where, and whether critical watch stations are manned. Restricted areas (engine room, bridge, cargo holds) must be enforced. Lone-worker monitoring and muster station headcount are non-negotiable.

Compliance Crew & Fleet Management

Crew shifts, certifications, and schedules managed through shipping ERP systems. Eyenet bridges the gap between shore-side crew modules and actual onboard presence — detecting mismatches and generating automated reports for captain, officers, and HQ.

Eyenet Presence — Crew Attendance & Muster

Dual-factor crew verification at every critical station — facial recognition plus QR/NFC badge — with automated attendance reports to captain and HQ.

Eyenet Presence is the backbone of onboard crew management. It assigns crew members to watch schedules and specific zones (bridge, engine control room, cargo office, galley), then tracks actual attendance against those schedules in real time. When a crew member fails to report for watch, the system alerts immediately — no one has to notice a missing person after the fact.

Maritime Use Cases

  • Watch changeover verification: Dual-factor check-in (QR/NFC badge + facial recognition) at bridge and engine control room. The system confirms the right person with the right certification is on watch — alerting the captain if a scheduled officer does not appear within the grace period.
  • Muster station headcount: During drills and actual emergencies, Presence operating in Area mode at each muster station provides real-time headcount — officers see instantly who is accounted for and who is missing, without manual roll calls.
  • ERP crew module sync: Crew manifest and watch schedules imported from the shipping company's ERP system. Presence compares scheduled vs actual attendance and flags discrepancies. Attendance records sync back to the ERP crew module when connected, providing shore-side HR with verified presence data.
  • Certification-aware alerts: If a crew member assigned to engine room watch lacks a valid STCW certification (per ERP data), the system alerts the chief engineer before the watch begins — preventing compliance violations.
  • Automated attendance reports: Daily, per-voyage, and per-shift attendance reports generated automatically and distributed to captain, department heads, and fleet managers at HQ.

Eyenet Gate — Gangway & Entrance Control

Real-time onboard/ashore headcount at every gangway. Track exactly who is on the vessel at all times — crew, visitors, contractors, and port officials.

Eyenet Gate monitors every entrance and exit point on the vessel — primarily gangways — providing real-time people counters, identity verification, and automated logging of all embarkation and disembarkation events. In an emergency, the captain has an instant, accurate headcount. During port calls, Gate tracks visitors and contractors separately from crew, ensuring no unauthorised personnel remain onboard after departure.

Maritime Use Cases

  • Gangway monitoring: Real-time count of who is onboard vs ashore. Every crew departure/return logged with timestamp and identity. Shore-leave tracking integrated with ERP crew module — crew status updated to "ashore" automatically.
  • Visitor & contractor tracking: During port calls, temporary personnel (stevedores, agents, surveyors, technicians) are logged with entry/exit timestamps. System alerts if a visitor has not disembarked by departure time.
  • Stowaway detection: Unrecognised faces at gangway or in restricted corridors trigger instant alerts to the security officer and captain — with the person's image and location attached.
  • Abandon-ship scenario: Gate mode combined with Presence at muster stations provides a full picture: who was last seen onboard, who has reached which muster station, and who is still unaccounted for — all in real time on the captain's dashboard.
  • Port state control readiness: Automated crew list generation with embarkation/disembarkation timestamps — ready for inspection by port authorities at any moment.

Eyenet Sentinel — Watch Station Vigilance Monitoring

Automatically detect when a watch station is unattended — bridge, engine control room, or security post — and alert the captain before it becomes a safety incident.

Eyenet Sentinel ensures that critical watch stations are never left unattended beyond a configurable threshold. It monitors the designated watchkeeper's presence at their post — on the bridge, in the engine control room, at the cargo control station, or at the security desk — and triggers escalating alerts if no activity is detected. This is not about surveillance; it is about preventing the single most common cause of maritime incidents: an unattended watch.

Maritime Use Cases

  • Bridge watch officer monitoring: Sentinel detects when the officer of the watch (OOW) has been absent from the bridge beyond the configured grace period (e.g., 2 minutes). First alert goes to the OOW's mobile device; if not resolved, escalates to captain and chief mate.
  • Engine room unmanned machinery space (UMS) monitoring: During UMS periods at night, Sentinel confirms that the duty engineer performs scheduled rounds at the expected intervals. Missed rounds trigger an alert to the chief engineer.
  • Security watch post monitoring: On vessels with dedicated security watches (common on passenger ships and high-risk routes), Sentinel ensures the security officer remains at their post — critical for ISPS Code compliance.
  • Customizable per-station parameters: Each watch station can have its own absence threshold, escalation chain, and active hours — bridge wing might have a tighter threshold than the cargo office.
  • Fatigue management integration: Combined with Presence attendance data, Sentinel can flag anomalous patterns — e.g., a crew member who has been on watch for more than the maximum permitted hours under MLC 2006 — and alert the captain before fatigue becomes a safety risk.

Eyenet Vradar — Security Patrol & Restricted Zone Protection

Wearable camera eyewear for onboard security patrols. Instant face recognition against crew manifest and persons-of-interest watchlists — all on-premises, no cloud.

Eyenet Vradar equips the vessel's security officer with wearable camera eyewear (goggles) that provides real-time face recognition and person-of-interest alerts while on patrol. The system cross-references every face detected against the onboard crew manifest and a configurable watchlist — identifying stowaways, unauthorised personnel, or crew members who should not be in a particular zone. All processing happens on the vessel's local EyeNet installation; no video leaves the ship.

Maritime Use Cases

  • Crew manifest verification on patrol: Security officer walks through crew areas, engine spaces, and cargo holds — every person encountered is silently verified against the crew manifest. Unrecognised faces trigger an instant audio alert to the officer's eyewear and a notification to the captain's dashboard.
  • Restricted zone enforcement: Define virtual perimeters around the bridge, engine control room, ammunition locker (if applicable), and cargo control. Any unauthorised presence triggers immediate alerts to the security officer and department head. The zone definitions are enforced 24/7 by the AI — no human monitoring required.
  • Stowaway search operations: During pre-departure searches mandated by ISPS Code, the security officer uses Vradar goggles to scan compartments. Any person not on the crew or visitor manifest generates an alert with location stamp — dramatically accelerating search procedures.
  • Port security coordination: Persons-of-interest data (e.g., from port facility security or INTERPOL notices) can be loaded as a watchlist before port calls. The system alerts if any person of interest is detected during embarkation or onboard.
  • Drone mode for large vessels: On tankers, bulk carriers, and passenger ships, Vradar Drone mode provides aerial surveillance of deck areas and cargo holds — covering spaces that are impractical for fixed cameras.

Automatic Notification Architecture

Three-tier notification system: onboard alerts (zero latency, no internet required), ship-to-HQ sync (via VPN when connected), and ERP crew module integration (bi-directional data flow).

1Onboard Notifications

Zero latency. No internet required. Local network only.

  • Watch officer absence → mobile push + bridge audible alarm
  • Restricted zone intrusion → security officer eyewear + captain dashboard
  • Muster station headcount → captain & chief mate dashboards
  • Unrecognised person at gangway → security officer + captain
  • Watch station unattended beyond threshold → escalating: officer → captain → chief engineer
  • Visitor not disembarked by departure → captain + security officer

2Ship-to-HQ Sync

Via VPN when docked. Via satellite for critical alerts at sea.

  • Daily crew attendance reports → fleet HR dashboard
  • Security events (stowaway, intrusion, POI detection) → Company Security Officer
  • Watch compliance exceptions → fleet operations manager
  • Crew certification expiry alerts → fleet HR & crewing department
  • Satellite-priority alerts (man overboard, fire, security breach) → HQ operations centre in real time
  • Degraded mode: all data queued locally at sea, auto-synced when VPN reconnects

3ERP Crew Module Integration

Bi-directional sync between Eyenet and shipping ERP systems.

  • ERP → Eyenet: Crew manifest, watch schedules, certifications, shore-leave authorisations
  • Eyenet → ERP: Actual attendance records, embarkation/disembarkation timestamps, watch compliance data, certification utilisation reports
  • Mismatch alerts: Crew member on ERP manifest but not detected onboard → alert to crewing department
  • Certification gap alerts: Crew assigned to watch requiring certification they don't hold (per ERP data) → alert before watch begins
  • Rest hours compliance: Eyenet tracks actual watch hours → ERP flags MLC 2006 rest hour violations → alert to captain and DPA

Notification Delivery Channels

Captain Dashboard Officer Mobile Push Email to HQ Audible/Visual Alarm ERP API Webhook Matrix Dashboard

All notifications are configurable per trigger: choose which channels, which recipients, and which escalation path for each event type. Critical safety alerts (man overboard, fire, abandon ship) bypass all filters and notify all channels simultaneously.

Key VPN Architecture Properties

  • Mesh topology: Every vessel can communicate directly with every other vessel and with HQ — no hub-and-spoke bottleneck. Queries from Matrix to vessels are parallel and direct.
  • ACL-based fleet isolation: Each shipping company's fleet is tagged with a unique Matrix group ID. EyeNet VPN ACLs cryptographically enforce that vessels in Fleet A cannot communicate with vessels in Fleet B — even though they share the same coordination server.
  • Zero public exposure: All inter-vessel and vessel-to-HQ communication travels over the EyeNet VPN encrypted tunnel. No EyeNet service port is exposed to the public internet. No cloud relay processes video or presence data.
  • Automatic enrollment: When a new vessel is commissioned, the node fetches a pre-auth key from the Authority, enrolls in the VPN mesh, and is automatically discoverable by the fleet Matrix — no manual network configuration required.
  • Degraded mode resilience: If a vessel goes offline (at sea), its local EyeNet installation continues operating fully. When the VPN reconnects (docking, satellite), all queued data synchronises automatically. Matrix reports mark offline vessels as "unreachable" rather than failing.

Technical Specifications

1 Mini PC Per Vessel

16–32 GB RAM, modern multi-core CPU, standard GPU. No specialised hardware or GPU servers required. Fanless, compact form factor suitable for shipboard installation. Power: standard 12–24V DC or 110–240V AC.

2–5 Cameras Per Vessel

Standard IP cameras, WiFi cams, or USB cameras. No proprietary hardware. Typical vessel deployment: gangway, bridge, engine control room, muster station, deck/cargo area. Expandable per vessel requirements.

100% Offline Capable

All core functions — presence tracking, face recognition, alerting, gate monitoring, watch vigilance — operate fully offline. Internet required only for HQ sync and remote notifications. Video and data never leave the vessel unless explicitly configured.

All Eyenet applications run on-premises with no cloud dependency for video or data. Each vessel operates a complete, self-contained installation. The VPN mesh provides encrypted fleet connectivity without exposing any service to the public internet. Core operation functions fully offline at sea.
