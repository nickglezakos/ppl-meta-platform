Executive Summary

A strategic technology cooperation focused on the joint design, development, and commercialization of an integration middleware connecting EyeNet's computer-vision platform with Talos Group's eHealth ecosystem.

The middleware acts as the intelligent bridge between the two platforms. It hosts connectors embedding business logic across both companies' systems, exposes fully tokenized (secured) endpoints, and publishes every capability as an MCP (Model Context Protocol) server — one per service — enabling AI agents and third-party systems to consume integrated vision + health workflows securely and uniformly.

EyeNet — on the one side, operating in headless mode, exposes computer-vision capabilities as machine-consumable services.

2.3  The Opportunity

Both companies operate in physically distributed, security-sensitive, connectivity-constrained environments, and both serve overlapping markets (maritime, defense, critical infrastructure). Today their systems operate in silos: EyeNet sees what happens on site; Talos systems care for people on site. A middleware that fuses real-time visual situational awareness with health operations creates entirely new product categories neither party can offer alone.

3.2  Core Components

EyeNet Headless Connectors

Consume EyeNet events and data streams — presence counts, gate crossings, area occupancy, camera events, demographic analytics, alert states — and abstract EyeNet's internal APIs behind stable middleware contracts so version upgrades remain transparent to Talos.

Talos REST API Connectors

Wrap Talos services (EMR/HIS records, IoMT vitals feeds, telemedicine session management, kiosk status, crew health records) behind uniform adapter interfaces, mapping Talos data models (patients, encounters, devices) to middleware domain objects.

Connector Logic & Orchestration Engine

Configurable business rules spanning both systems, with an event-driven core (publish/subscribe) plus synchronous request/response paths.

Middleware Dashboard & Connectors Framework

An administration & operations dashboard visualizes and manages every connector. Each connector embeds its own trigger-&-action logic, modeled on the EyeNet platform, keeping system-to-system interoperability fully decoupled from the MCP servers.

Tokenization & Security Layer

Every endpoint tokenized — no direct system-to-system calls; OAuth 2.0 / JWT scoped tokens with short TTLs, per-client/per-service issuance, full data-flow audit trail aligned with Talos's ISO 27001 posture, and fully offline/on-premises operation.

MCP Server per Service

Each middleware service is published as its own MCP server in a standardized, agent-ready interface. Examples: mcp-presence, mcp-gate-events, mcp-patient-vitals, mcp-telemedicine-session, mcp-kiosk-dispatch.

Joint ML Model Development & R&D

Beyond integration, the cooperation includes the joint development of dedicated machine-learning models trained on the combined data acquired by the sensors of both systems.

5.1  Vision

The fusion of machine-vision output (presence, movement, gate/area events, demographics, visual activity patterns) with telemedicine & inpatient sensory output (vitals from IoMT devices, patient monitoring streams, EMR/HIS events, Talos REST events) creates a unique, previously unavailable dataset. Insights emerge exactly at this intersection — correlations between physical behavior and physiological state, early-warning signals, and operational patterns neither system can detect alone.

5.2  Example Research Directions

Deterioration prediction — combining vitals trends with mobility/activity observed by vision.

Fall-risk & recovery scoring — fusing gait/movement analysis with biometric data.

Crowd/density health analytics — for wards, vessels, and isolated facilities.

Anomaly detection — across fused event streams (vision × API × device telemetry).

Behavioral baselining — crew & patient safety in maritime environments.

5.3  R&D vs. Direct Development Split

Direct Development Track

Models addressing immediately evident insights (occupancy-driven alerting, presence-correlated kiosk activation, simple threshold/fusion rules) are developed and productized within the standard roadmap, delivering near-term value.

R&D Track

Novel hypotheses requiring experimentation, dataset curation, model training, and clinical/domain validation run as structured R&D, with jointly agreed success criteria, milestones, and potential co-funded research programs (e.g., national/EU funding instruments).

Both tracks share the same data pipeline and governance framework established by the middleware, ensuring a smooth transition of validated R&D results into production connectors and MCP services.

5.4  Data Governance

All ML work operates under strict data-governance rules consistent with GDPR, medical-device regulations, and both parties' certifications — anonymization/pseudonymization at the middleware boundary, on-premises processing aligned with EyeNet's zero-cloud principle, and explicit consent/role-based access for any patient-related data.

Cooperation Model

Joint Development

Shared technical working group. EyeNet leads headless adapters and vision-domain connectors; Talos leads REST API connectors and healthcare compliance; joint ownership of the orchestration and MCP layers.

IP Framework

Each party retains IP in its own platform; the middleware is jointly developed under a mutually agreed joint-IP or dual-license arrangement (detailed in the definitive agreement).

Go-to-Market

Co-branded offering spanning maritime, healthcare, defense, and critical-infrastructure customers; joint pilots with existing clients of both parties.

Commercial Structure

Revenue share or reseller/licensing model for the middleware and bundled solutions — subject to negotiation after the pilot phase.
