## Executive Summary

EyeNet should be operated and sold through a structured B2B channel model, not as a simple direct software product with a public universal price.

At the highest level:

- the software company acts as the **licensor** and sells the software licence
- the **distributor** applies policy, governs the channel, and builds the reseller network
- the **reseller** owns the end-customer commercial offer and delivers the customer-facing project
- **support** protects technical continuity, diagnosis, and recovery during deployment and live operations

This operating model means that EyeNet should be treated as a channel-governed software licensing business with customized customer offers.

The final end-customer proposal should normally include more than software. It should combine software licensing, use-case analysis, deployment planning, hardware prerequisites, implementation, training, and installation services.

This approach is closer to mature enterprise channel ecosystems such as Microsoft or Cisco in structure, while remaining adapted to EyeNet's product and deployment reality.

## Core Principle

The roles in EyeNet should be treated as real business and operational responsibilities across the customer lifecycle, not only as software access levels.

At a high level:

- `admin` owns governance, policy, commercial control, and exceptional decisions
- `distributor` owns channel coordination, partner scaling, and operational supervision across reseller networks
- `reseller` owns the direct customer relationship and the final customer offer
- `support` owns technical diagnosis, continuity, and recovery

These roles work together around one customer lifecycle, but they do not serve the same purpose.

## Business Role Definitions

### Admin

The admin role acts as the governance and authority layer of the business.

Admin is responsible for:

- setting commercial and operational policy
- defining offer boundaries and exception rules
- governing channel structure and business rules
- approving exceptional cases such as suspension, reinstatement, or unusual commercial terms
- handling escalations that exceed distributor or reseller authority

Admin should operate as the control plane of the business, not as the default actor for everyday customer execution.

### Distributor

The distributor role acts as the scaling and channel-governance layer.

Distributor is responsible for:

- applying licensor policy in a market, territory, or commercial segment
- recruiting, organizing, and supervising resellers
- improving channel execution quality
- coordinating larger opportunities or multi-partner delivery patterns
- escalating non-standard cases and protecting pricing discipline
- monitoring onboarding and account-health quality across multiple reseller relationships

Distributor should be understood as the channel multiplier, not mainly as the installation contractor.

### Reseller

The reseller role acts as the direct commercial owner of the customer relationship.

Reseller is responsible for:

- leading the customer opportunity from presales to signed project
- understanding customer use cases and deployment needs
- shaping the final commercial offer
- managing onboarding and go-live progression with the customer
- coordinating installation, training, and project delivery
- maintaining post-go-live account momentum and relationship continuity

Reseller should be treated as the main customer-facing business owner.

### Support

The support role acts as the technical continuity and recovery function.

Support is responsible for:

- diagnosing technical issues
- protecting service continuity
- guiding recovery and remediation
- distinguishing product defects from deployment issues or misuse
- producing technical findings that help the commercial roles manage accounts responsibly

Support should not own the commercial relationship, but it must strengthen the roles that do.

## Lifecycle View

The role model becomes clearer when mapped to lifecycle phases.

### Presales

Presales is about qualification, fit, opportunity shaping, and readiness.

At a high level:

- `admin` defines offer boundaries and exception rules
- `distributor` aligns the right channel path and partner ownership
- `reseller` leads discovery, use-case understanding, and customer qualification
- `support` contributes where technical feasibility or deployment complexity must be clarified

### Sales

Sales is about commercial closure and a supportable commitment.

At a high level:

- `admin` governs exceptional pricing and strategic approvals
- `distributor` manages channel coordination and larger commercial structures
- `reseller` owns the final customer proposal and commercial closure
- `support` may validate technical assumptions that materially affect delivery risk

### Onboarding

Onboarding is where the sold account becomes an activated and operational account.

At a high level:

- `admin` defines the approved lifecycle model and handles special approvals
- `distributor` monitors onboarding quality across its partner network
- `reseller` owns the customer-facing onboarding journey and readiness checks
- `support` resolves technical blockers during activation, installation, or deployment

### Operations Events

Operations events include normal post-go-live commercial or operational changes.

At a high level:

- `admin` governs policy-sensitive actions
- `distributor` monitors systemic channel risks or recurring issues
- `reseller` handles customer-facing coordination for routine changes
- `support` handles the technical implications of those changes

### Problem Solving

Problem solving includes incidents, adoption blockers, ambiguous cases, and operational failures.

At a high level:

- `admin` handles policy-level resolution when a case becomes exceptional
- `distributor` coordinates cross-account or cross-partner resolution where needed
- `reseller` owns customer-facing accountability and expectation management
- `support` owns diagnosis, evidence, and technical recovery guidance

### Customer Success

Customer success is about value realization, retention, and durable account health.

At a high level:

- `admin` defines high-level success rules and governance
- `distributor` monitors portfolio-level performance patterns
- `reseller` owns the ongoing business relationship and growth path
- `support` contributes operational insight into friction and adoption blockers

### Churn Management

Churn management includes renewal risk, dissatisfaction, inactivity, and managed offboarding.

At a high level:

- `admin` sets policy for suspension, recovery, reinstatement, or termination
- `distributor` assesses broader channel consequences and escalations
- `reseller` owns the retention or transition conversation with the customer
- `support` clarifies the technical reality and remaining recovery options

## Problematic Customer Cases

### Customer Suspension

When a customer must be suspended because of non-payment, misuse, policy breach, or operational conflict:

- `admin` should own the governing policy or final decision
- `distributor` should coordinate where channel-wide or territory consequences exist
- `reseller` should manage the customer-facing communication and structured resolution path
- `support` should execute any technical containment or continuity action required

Suspension should be treated as a business-controlled action with technical consequences.

### Misuse Or Problematic Use

When a customer creates risk through misuse, repeated policy violations, or unsupported operating behavior:

- `admin` defines what is unacceptable and what escalation paths are valid
- `distributor` evaluates whether the pattern is isolated or systemic
- `reseller` works directly with the customer to correct or reframe usage
- `support` documents technical symptoms, impact, and operational boundaries

### Escalated Service Failure

When repeated or high-impact service failure affects the customer:

- `admin` may approve exceptions, credits, or special handling
- `distributor` may coordinate broader remediation if multiple accounts are involved
- `reseller` remains responsible for trust and customer continuity
- `support` leads technical triage and evidence-based remediation

## Commercial Model

EyeNet should be priced and sold as a channel-delivered B2B solution, not as a universal public software SKU.

The commercial model should be based on three layers:

- the **licensor** sells the software licence and defines channel and product policy
- the **distributor** applies channel policy and governs the reseller ecosystem
- the **reseller** packages and sells the final customer offer

This means the end-customer commercial proposal should usually be customized and solution-based.

---

## What The Licensor Sells

The software company should sell only the software licence and the right to use the EyeNet products under defined commercial terms.

At a high level, the licensor should own:

- software licensing model
- licence packaging rules
- channel discount logic
- product and bundle eligibility rules
- support-policy boundaries
- renewal and upgrade policy
- commercial and brand governance

The licensor should not normally be the contracting party for the full end-customer implementation project.

---

## What The Distributor Does Commercially

The distributor should act as the policy and network layer of the channel.

At a high level, the distributor should:

- apply licensor pricing and channel policy
- recruit and structure a reseller network
- supervise reseller quality and commercial discipline
- coordinate larger or strategic opportunities
- escalate non-standard pricing or approval cases
- standardize execution expectations across its market

---

## What The Reseller Sells

The reseller should own the final customer contract and the customer-facing offer.

That offer may include:

- software licence as part of the solution
- presales discovery and qualification
- customized study of use cases
- project scoping and deployment design
- hardware prerequisite assessment such as PCs, laptops, tablets, displays, cameras, networking, edge devices, or future specialized hardware
- implementation services
- hardware and software installation
- configuration and rollout support
- customer training
- post-go-live coordination

The end-customer price should therefore be solution-led and project-led, not only licence-led.

## Pricing Structure

The pricing model should be structured in layers.

### Channel Economics Clarification

The commercial flow should normally work through channel pricing rather than equal direct pricing for every party.

At a high level:

- the software company sets the base licence price, channel policy, and discount rules
- the distributor receives a channel discount or protected margin because it carries channel-governance and scaling responsibility
- the reseller also receives channel pricing so it can build the final customer offer and preserve delivery margin
- the distributor will normally have a stronger pricing position than the reseller when it carries broader channel obligations

Services revenue should usually be handled separately from the software licence.

At a high level:

- installation, onboarding, training, local support, and project execution revenue should normally belong to the reseller
- for high-value, strategic, or more complex accounts, the distributor may also participate directly in services revenue
- deep product escalation, defect handling, and policy-sensitive technical recovery remain governed upstream even when frontline service is commercialized by the reseller

This keeps software economics, channel incentives, and project-delivery revenue clearly separated.

### 1. Software Licence Layer

This is the licensor-controlled software charge into the channel.

At a high level, it may vary by:

- product family
- deployment mode
- installation size
- camera count or monitored points
- use-case complexity
- edition or bundle level
- annual or multi-year term

### 2. Channel Commercial Layer

This is the margin and governance layer applied through distributor and reseller structure.

At a high level, it should cover:

- distributor margin
- reseller margin
- partner-tier treatment
- protected opportunity or deal-registration logic
- strategic-account handling
- non-standard discount approval rules

### 3. Project Services Layer

This is the reseller-led implementation and project layer.

At a high level, it should cover:

- site study
- use-case analysis
- deployment design
- installation effort
- system configuration
- hardware integration
- training
- rollout support
- optional managed services

### 4. Hardware Layer

Hardware should normally remain separate from the software licence.

At a high level, it may include:

- cameras
- laptops or PCs
- tablets
- displays
- local servers or edge computers
- networking and accessories
- future specialized hardware such as smart-glasses-based kits for Security Officer Agent

This layer should usually be priced and contracted by the reseller as part of the project solution.

Based on the current product roadmap, pricing should be organized by product family and deployment profile, not by one flat universal platform fee.

At a high level, the pricing model should accommodate:

- Intelligent Signage
- Underage Detector

The packaging model may evolve around:

- single-product offers
- vertical bundles
- multi-site enterprise bundles
- fixed-installation packages
- mobile-operation packages
- premium security or compliance packages

This keeps pricing aligned with how customers actually buy solutions.

## Customer Offer Philosophy

For higher-value B2B customers, the final offer should normally be customized.

That offer should reflect:

- the operational use case
- the number of sites or monitored zones
- deployment mode
- hardware environment
- implementation complexity
- training requirements
- service expectations
- customer scale and strategic importance

EyeNet should therefore avoid being positioned primarily as a public-price self-service catalogue in these channel-led cases.

---

## Agreement Structure Direction

The agreement structure should follow the familiar three-level enterprise channel pattern.

At a high level:

- **Licensor-Distributor Agreement**: defines licence rights, commercial policy, channel obligations, territory or market rules, governance, branding, and reporting
- **Distributor-Reseller Agreement**: defines reseller enablement, sales rights, margin structure, service expectations, and channel-policy compliance
- **Reseller-End Customer Agreement**: defines the final project scope, commercial terms, implementation, hardware scope, services, support handling, and deployment obligations

This keeps software ownership, channel governance, and customer delivery responsibilities clearly separated.

---

## Recommended High-Level Position

The recommended business position is:

- EyeNet is a **licensed software platform sold through the channel**
- the licensor controls the licence model and channel rules
- the distributor governs and scales the partner ecosystem
- the reseller owns the final customer offer and project contract
- support ensures technical continuity and recovery across the lifecycle
- the final customer price is a **customized business offer** built around software, project services, hardware, training, and deployment

The key principle is simple:

EyeNet should be sold as a channel-governed software licensing business with reseller-led customized customer offers, not as a one-size-fits-all publicly priced software product.

## Addendum: Example Use Case For Annual Licence Allocation

The following example is illustrative only and is included to make the channel economics easier to understand in practice. It is not a fixed commercial policy.

### Example Scenario

- annual customer contract: `20,000`
- scope: software licences only
- no installation, training, support, or hardware included

### Example Allocation

A reasonable mid-market international channel assumption would be:

- **Licensor**: `13,000` or `65%`
- **Distributor**: `2,000` or `10%`
- **Reseller**: `5,000` or `25%`

### Example List Pricing Model

The same example can also be expressed through a list-pricing model.

- **List Price**: `25,000`
- **Illustrative End-Customer Contract Value**: `20,000`
- **Illustrative Floor Price**: `18,500`

Illustrative channel logic:

- distributor pricing position reflects a stronger channel discount because of governance and scaling responsibility
- reseller pricing position leaves room for customer acquisition and commercial ownership
- discounts below the floor price should require licensor approval

This makes it possible to preserve channel margin while protecting licence value and price discipline.
